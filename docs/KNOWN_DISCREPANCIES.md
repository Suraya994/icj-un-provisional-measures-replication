# Known discrepancies and resolved audit questions

## 1. Published P5 wording — genuine wording error

The published paper states twice that **six of the 24 primary orders name the Russian Federation as respondent**. The case titles in the legal corpus support: Russian Federation = 3 orders, United States of America = 2, France = 1. Thus **six** is the count for *all permanent-member respondents*, not Russia alone.

The descriptive split `0/6` permanent-member-respondent orders versus `2/18` other orders is unchanged. This does not affect the 2/24 headline result, but the wording should be corrected through an erratum or correction note.

## 2. Recall breakdown 113 / 5 / 1 / 1 — aggregate statement not row-reproducible

The paper reports that the 120 additional candidates comprised 113 routine ICJ judicial-election items, 5 routine closed President briefings, 1 Avena item and 1 S/PV.9756 item. The exact 120 candidate pair IDs are reconstructable, and the system contains a 120-row binary human-review ledger confirming the manuscript-supported outcome that all 120 were reviewed and none was order-specific positive. What remains missing is the original **four-way human row-to-subcategory mapping** that produced the aggregate 113/5/1/1 statement.

Automated metadata labels do not reproduce that breakdown and must not be substituted for the lost human ledger. The public package therefore preserves the published aggregate statement separately and labels the row-level candidate file as reconstructed.

## 3. S/PV.9638 — audit concern resolved

This is **not** treated as a discrepancy. The official meeting record at p. 2 explicitly reports the ICJ order of 24 May 2024 to the Security Council, and later interventions engage the order. The paper's classification as order-specific **deliberative engagement**, not adopted collective action, is supported.

## 4. Review coverage — interpretation tightened

The original 233 layer covers 209 of 4,868 post-order (0–180 day) pairs in the 24-order primary sample. The reconstructed recall candidate set adds review evidence for 33 additional primary post-order pairs. Consequently, a zero order-level engagement outcome must not be described as proof that every eligible UN record was read. Coverage files are now public.

## 5. Article 41(2) exposure

Published windows use order date as time zero. Post-publication source checking confirms Article 41(2) transmission documents for the three 2024 Case No. 192 orders (S/2024/116, S/2024/549, S/2024/411). These are added as a separate enrichment registry and are not used to rewrite the published timing analysis.
